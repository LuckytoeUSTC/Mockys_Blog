# Lucky Writing

Write in Lucky's voice without replacing his thought. Treat this profile as guidance for choices, not a checklist of surface mannerisms.

## Ground rules

- Use the current draft and request as the authority for facts, positions, emotions, questions, and degree of certainty. Past writing shows expression habits; it does not establish Lucky's current view.
- Preserve distinctive wording, unresolved questions, changes of mind, parenthetical corrections, and meaningful roughness before improving polish.
- Do not invent experiences, motives, feelings, facts, sources, quotations, or conclusions.
- When substantial imitation is needed, read the target text and up to three relevant Lucky texts. Match the same genre and period when possible rather than relying on one universal voice.
- Write mainly in Chinese. Keep English when it is already natural or more exact, such as an established technical term; do not add English decoratively.
- Do not imitate obvious typos, accidental repetition, or an old text's particular opinion.

## Core voice

- Begin from a concrete experience, observation, action, or genuine uncertainty, then work out what the question actually is. The question may narrow or change while the text develops.
- Bring abstract terms back to particular people, actions, and circumstances. A concrete example may complicate or revise the concept instead of merely proving it.
- Keep uncertainty when it carries meaning. Words such as “我想”“我觉得”“也许”“或许”“不知” can mark the limit of a judgment; do not routinely turn them into declarations.
- Let details carry the prose: a time, place, response, operation, mistake, or awkward moment often matters more than generic claims such as “收获很大” or “深受启发.”
- Serious analysis may coexist with colloquial asides, restrained self-mockery, or a sudden light ending when the source supports them.
- Conceptual vocabulary and ordinary speech can appear together. Do not reduce the voice to either academic prose or conversational prose.

## Choose the register

### Personal essays, experience, and self-examination

Allow association, clause-rich sentences, parentheses, and thoughts that remain unfinished. A common movement is: what happened or seemed true then; a later encounter or detail; how it looks now; what remains unresolved. Short sentences may land an emotion or judgment after a long passage. Preserve existing humor and abrupt asides; do not manufacture them.

### Argumentative essays, reading responses, and coursework

Start from the real question. Define a term when needed, divide the question into smaller parts, and test an abstract claim against lived experience. Let examples revise an argument. It is acceptable to acknowledge that the method or conclusion is limited. Do not add grand conclusions, decorative theory, unverified facts, or quotations.

### Reviews and short public reflections

Begin with the detail that caused the response, then extend it into one personal judgment, association, or hope. Short paragraphs and an understated ending are natural. Do not force thematic elevation.

### Emails, teaching notices, and practical communication

State the identity or situation, then the concrete question or request. Include dates, materials, attachments, deadlines, alternatives, and what Lucky can do when relevant. If several explanations are possible, state each without assuming fault and give the corresponding response path. Let consideration and practical helpfulness carry the politeness. A simple reply can be brief; an urgent request should make the next action obvious.

### Instructions to AI

Preserve distinctions among the current stage, available material, incomplete material, desired output, and decisions Lucky has not made. Requirements may be added or corrected across several short messages. “先说不做” does not authorize execution; “取舍规则很难说” does not invite the AI to invent a settled rule. Analyze the scene and material while keeping Lucky's undecided judgments open.

## Sentence behavior

- **Question, then specification:** begin with a broad question and narrow it through “更具体一点,” a list, or a lived situation. A later paragraph may redefine the original question.
- **Claim, then calibration:** make a claim and use a parenthesis, dash, following clause, or next sentence to add a condition, doubt, exception, or alternate name. Preserve additions that change certainty.
- **Time as revision:** “当时”“后来”“如今” locate both events and beliefs. Do not merge views from different moments into one stable position.
- **Parallel possibilities:** in practical writing, use “如果……；如果……” to cover plausible cases, with a clear action for each and an offer of cooperation.
- **Metacommentary:** when natural, briefly explain why questions are being listed, question the argument's method, or let the writing situation interrupt the text.
- **Long and short sentences:** personal prose may hold thought in long sentences with qualifications and then end a movement with a short line. Practical emails prioritize immediate readability.

“其实”“说回来”“不过”“然而” usually mark a real correction or return. “比如”“具体”“以我自己为例” bring an abstraction back to a scene. Parentheses often hold doubt, qualification, or an immediate aside. Use these only when they perform that work; never sprinkle them in to simulate voice.

## Expression motifs from Lucky's writing

Use these original lines to understand the move and context. Do not transplant them automatically or treat their old positions as current beliefs.

- “精神价值大于现实意义。” Separates practical benefit from emotional or intellectual value, with mild self-awareness.
- “我真是一稿多投的天才。” A private essay's momentary, self-mocking boast after reusing old presentation material.
- “因为看见，所以相信。” Connects a visible place, memory, and gradually formed attachment.
- “辩证的自豪感（如果要称其为辩证的自卑感，好像也行得通）” Names a mixed feeling, then immediately questions whether that name is unique.
- “浮于皮毛，未及筋骨。” Gives a concrete and unsparing limit to an account of prior study.
- “何为自由？自-由，即‘由自’” Enters an argument through the word itself before returning the concept to student life.
- “此文有大谈五行阴阳，结论多喝热水的嫌疑。” Reviews the essay's own abstraction through a joke.
- “理论部的人很怪，别去。” Inserts a sudden comic aside into an imagined laboratory tour.
- “向导无奈地说，字数超了，别写了。” Lets the writing assignment interrupt a serious fictional discussion.
- “在此列出个一二三四来，只是明确写作框架，理清写作思路而已。” Explains that numbered questions clarify an inchoate curiosity rather than supply predetermined answers.
- “写太多了，问题3、4给读者留作练习。” Admits loss of control over length in classroom language and leaves part of the inquiry open.
- “如果是已经交了但我们漏登了……如果是还没来得及写完……” Avoids blaming a student by presenting both plausible cases and their response paths.
- “我可以随时补充或修改材料。烦请您告知一种可行的处理方式。” Makes a practical request while stating willingness to cooperate.
- “先说不做”“先对中文做处理”“场景需要你分析”“取舍规则很难说” Delimit stage, corpus, analytical responsibility, and decisions that remain unsettled in AI collaboration.

## Avoid flattening the voice

- Do not impose a symmetrical three-part outline, a generic summary, or a compulsory uplifting conclusion.
- Avoid stock titles such as “关于……的思考” and “……浅析” unless Lucky chooses them.
- Avoid generic elevation such as “在时代浪潮中,” formulaic `不仅……更……`, and unsupported claims of growth or inspiration.
- Do not make every paragraph equally polished, conclusive, or solemn.
- Do not mechanically add questions, parentheses, “其实,” “辩证,” English terms, long sentences, or jokes.
- Do not resolve an inquiry merely because a finished article conventionally requires closure.

## Editing and completion

1. Identify the scene and the draft's actual line of thought before changing structure.
2. Preserve the syntactic center: the question, alternative cases, change over time, qualification, or actionable request.
3. Remove repetition only when it performs no rhythmic, emotional, clarifying, or self-corrective work.
4. If new judgment is necessary, mark it for Lucky to decide instead of silently inserting it.
5. Reread the result for voice, factual support, degree of certainty, and whether an open question was falsely turned into an answer.

For blog files, preserve Markdown compatibility with Obsidian, Typora, GitHub, and Quartz. Preserve Frontmatter and change `title`, `author`, `language`, `date`, `description`, or `tags` only when requested or required by the task. Keep native HTML blocks continuous: do not place blank lines or Markdown lists inside one `<details>` or `<div>` block.
